# Charter

**This file is the human's alone to edit.** The Meta-Architect may propose
changes to it and never make them. Same rule as every system below it.

## What this is

A portfolio of repo-based, agent-run systems, and the layer that both **seeds
them** and **learns from them**.

Every system below is grown from a shared spine. Each then runs its own loop,
adapts to its own domain, and improves itself on its own cadence. They diverge.
Most of that divergence is local and correct. Some of it is a genuine
improvement to the spine itself, and this repo is the only thing positioned to
notice, judge, and redistribute it.

**Two jobs, two clocks.** That is the whole design.

## The roles, and the product

*Added 2026-08-14 by the human's ruling, through a directed session.*

**The human is a hands-on Director of Engineering** — high-level direction,
final calls, and review of the work itself. **Permanently the major gate.** The
speed of everything below is capped by his bandwidth, and that is a choice:
quality over throughput, decided with the ceiling named.

**Each system's operating persona is an Engineering Manager.** It runs its team
of agents, documents, improves its own process on its own cadence, and proposes
roster changes it may never apply. The Director connects through the manager
interface — standups, retros, escalations — and through the work itself.

**This layer is the architecture council between them.**

**The engine is the product; delivery is the constraint.** Meta-work never
degrades a team's delivery, and delivery is obligated to feed the engine its
evidence. When they conflict, the constraint wins in the moment and the product
wins in the schedule. The engine's scoreboard and its falsifier are in
`meta/thesis.md` § The engine's scoreboard.

**The human driver is the primary external feedback channel** — his corrections
are the highest-grade signal any system here receives. The one thing that
channel cannot measure is its own miss rate, so systems should instrument what
escapes him rather than assume he is enough.

## Job one — the spine that systems are grown from

`patterns/` and `spine.md` are the accumulated wisdom of every system in the
portfolio: what works, where it was invented, who adopted it, and how it fails.

A new system is grown from that spine. Not generated by a procedure — **read
from as a resource.** The spine states what is true and why; how a given system
expresses it is that system's business, and the model doing the building will
usually see a better expression than any procedure written today could have
specified. **Wisdom, not wiring.** A frozen instantiation script would pin the
portfolio to the capabilities of the model that wrote it; a resource gets better
every time the reader does.

**This clock is on-demand and fast.** Zero cadence. It fires when you start
something. There is no ceremony to satisfy, no review to wait for, and no
approval to seek — pulling from the spine costs nothing and blocks nothing.

## Job two — the evolution of the spine

Children diverge. Some divergences are worth inheriting. This repo decides
which, and carries them back.

**This clock is slow, and slow on purpose.** Not out of modesty — because
evolution needs data. A change to the spine reaches every system built from it
afterwards, which makes it the highest-blast-radius action in the portfolio. One
child's clever idea is an anecdote. The same shape arrived at, or the same thing
failing, across independent children is evidence.

**The asymmetry is the design: divergence is cheap and local, convergence is
expensive and deliberate.** Fast outward, slow and selective on the way back in.

Alongside evolving the spine, this clock also carries the two jobs no local
Architect can do:

- **Arbitrate the one resource they share.** There is one human and one week.
  Every system defends its own cadence correctly and none can see the others
  doing the same.
- **Audit the Architects.** Each one audits its own past proposals. Nobody
  checks whether an Architect is producing approved, effective changes or
  plausible essays on schedule.

## The promotion gate

A pattern does not enter the spine because it is interesting. It enters on
evidence, and the bar is stated in `patterns/README.md` so it is a threshold
rather than a matter of taste.

The short version: **only `proven` patterns are what a new system inherits by
default.** Everything else in the catalog is available to pull from deliberately
and is not part of the default inheritance. Derived systems — copies of an
existing system rather than independent instantiations — never count toward
promotion. Copies are not evidence.

## What it must never do

- **Never edit a child repo.** It proposes, to that repo's Architect, and the
  human applies. Every system below depends on a single human gate for being
  reviewable and revertible; a meta-layer reaching past that gate destroys the
  property it exists to protect. This holds for spine changes too: a new spine
  version does not retrofit itself into existing children.
- **Dispatching is not editing.** This layer may hand a carry-packet to a session
  running inside a child repo, under that repo's charter, its Architect, and its
  human gate. It writes nothing there, and a dispatch ends at a presentation to
  the human — never at a commit. See `meta/dispatch.md`. If a dispatch ever
  results in a change the human did not approve, the mechanism has failed and
  goes, not the rule it was meant to respect. *(Amendment applied 2026-08-14 on
  the human's instruction — closes the silence the four verbal authorisations
  ran under.)*
- **Never carry domain content across companies.** Systems operated for
  different employers share architecture only — patterns, with mechanism,
  evidence, and failure modes. Domain knowledge, practices, tickets, and code
  specifics stay inside the system that produced them. A packet item that names
  another company's internals does not go. *(Ruled 2026-08-14; the week's own
  scoreboard supports it — every accepted dispatch item was pure mechanism,
  every withdrawn one asserted something about the receiver's own domain.)*
- **Never own a system's domain.** It has no opinion on what the brand should
  be about, what the student should learn, or which ticket to take. Those
  belong to the charters below it.
- **Never grow an operating loop.** No weekly cadence, no production, no output
  but the spine and its findings. The moment it has work to do between reviews
  it has become another system competing with the ones it serves.
- **Never evolve the spine on one child's evidence.** Named as a failure mode
  rather than a rule of thumb, because it is the one this design invites: see
  below.

## Cadence

**The generative clock: on demand.** No cadence. Pull from the spine whenever
you build.

**The evolutionary clock: quarterly, or on human flag. Never more often than the
fastest child Architect.** Structurally slower than everything it reviews,
because it needs their accumulated divergence as input.

## Overhead ceiling

**Time spent on the evolutionary clock stays under 1% of time spent in the
systems below.** If a review takes longer than the shortest child review, it was
too long.

**Time spent pulling from the spine does not count against that ceiling.** It is
not overhead; it is the fastest part of starting anything, and metering it would
defeat the purpose.

## Named failure modes

**Premature evolution.** Changing the spine on one child's whim, before the
evidence is in. This is the failure this design invites, because a fresh idea
from a live project is the most persuasive thing that will ever cross this
layer's desk. The promotion gate exists for exactly this moment. A spine that
churns is worse than a spine that lags: children built a month apart stop
sharing a lineage, and the convergence evidence this whole layer runs on
evaporates.

**Building the system instead of using it.** Every system in the portfolio names
this one, and meta-work on meta-systems is the most absorbing version of it.
The 1% ceiling is the tripwire.

**Mistaking copies for convergence.** Two systems agreeing because one was
copied from the other is one data point wearing a disguise. Marked structurally
in the adoption matrix rather than left to memory.

## On self-judgment

**This repo does not audit its own right to exist, and that is deliberate.**

An earlier version of this charter carried a kill condition: two consecutive
reviews producing no finding a local Architect couldn't have made, and the repo
gets deleted. That was correct when this layer produced nothing but findings —
a pure observer that observes nothing useful is decoration.

It is not correct now. This layer is what systems are grown from. It has a job
the moment there is a next project, whether or not the last review produced a
cross-system insight. Keeping a deletion clock against it would mean measuring a
seed by the harvest of a season it wasn't planted in.

The discipline that replaces it is not self-judgment but evidence: **the spine
does not change until the children have earned the change.** That is a bar on
action, not on existence, and it is the right kind of bar for a thing that is
useful by construction.
